Reading the numbers

  • Recall: of the sentences or paragraphs that contain any risky clause, the share ClauseGuard flags. This is the number that matters most: a missed clause is the costliest mistake.
  • Precision: of the flags ClauseGuard raises, the share that annotators also marked.
  • Macro F1: the balance of precision and recall, averaged over categories, so rare categories count as much as common ones.
  • Document-level F1: whether each category is found anywhere in the document, which is what a reader usually wants to know.
Contracts are the hardest case. CUAD's annotations aren't exhaustive: real clauses that were repeated, or sat in attachments, were often left unmarked, and the model is penalised for finding them. Training a different encoder, adding twice as much data and oversampling rare clause types all ended at the same recall, so reaching 85% would need better annotations rather than more training.

Document-type detector

A TF-IDF and logistic regression model over windows of each document, trained on all three datasets plus news (AG News) and encyclopedia text (WikiText) as examples of unrelated text.

  • Full test documents: 100% correct.
  • Excerpts of 100–200 words: 99.4% correct.
  • Unrelated texts in the test set (news, encyclopedia articles): never confidently labelled as a legal document.
