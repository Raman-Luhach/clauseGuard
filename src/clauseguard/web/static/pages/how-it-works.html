How it works

From a long document to the clauses that matter.

ClauseGuard combines a document-type detector, three specialised classifiers and a scoring step. Here is what happens to your document, step by step.

The pipeline

Every document goes through the same six steps. On an ordinary laptop the whole thing takes about five seconds for a 5,000-word terms of service or contract.

ReadPDF text with page numbers, or plain text
CleanFix line breaks, hyphenation and spacing
SplitSentences, or paragraphs for privacy policies
ClassifyA fine-tuned transformer for the document type
ScoreSeverity-weighted, compared with similar documents
ExplainA plain-language note for every flag

Detecting the document type

Before anything is classified, a small, fast model reads the text and suggests whether it is a terms of service, a privacy policy or a contract. It was trained on full documents from all three datasets, plus news and encyclopedia text as examples of what is none of them.

The suggestion is only a suggestion: it pre-selects the type, and you can always change it. If the detector is unsure (below 60% confidence) or the text looks unrelated, ClauseGuard asks you to choose instead of guessing.

On held-out test documents the detector picks the right type for 100% of full documents and 99.4% of short excerpts of 100–200 words.

Finding risky clauses

Each document type has its own model, trained at the level its dataset was annotated: terms of service and contracts sentence by sentence, privacy policies paragraph by paragraph. A clause can match more than one category.

  • Terms of service: LEGAL-BERT, a BERT model pre-trained on legal text, fine-tuned on UNFAIR-ToS (50 terms of service annotated by legal experts).
  • Privacy policies: PrivBERT, pre-trained on privacy policies, fine-tuned on OPP-115 (115 policies annotated by law students).
  • Contracts: LEGAL-BERT fine-tuned on CUAD (510 commercial contracts annotated under the supervision of experienced lawyers), on 19 of the clause types lawyers check first.

Each category has its own decision threshold, tuned on validation data. For terms of service and contracts the thresholds favour recall: missing a risky clause costs you more than reading one extra flag.

To stay fast, a lightweight keyword model first screens every sentence; the transformer reads only those with some chance of a flag. On validation data this keeps more than 99% of the transformer's own flags while reading about 35–45% of the sentences.

The risk score

Every category has a severity: High Medium Low. Each risky category found counts once, weighted by its severity and by how confident the model is, so a long document isn't penalised for repeating itself. Clauses that work in your favour are shown as Protective and add nothing to the score. The result is calibrated so that a typical document of the same type scores 50.

  • 0–33, low: milder than most documents of its kind.
  • 34–66, medium: typical; read the high-severity flags.
  • 67–100, high: more aggressive than most; read carefully before agreeing.

For privacy policies, ClauseGuard also lists the protections the policy contains, such as how to access or delete your data, and the ones it does not mention; missing protections raise the score.

Explanations

Every category comes with a plain-language explanation of what such a clause usually means for you. In privacy policies, negations are taken into account: “we do not sell your personal information” is recognised as a reassurance and given a lower severity. A server can optionally use Claude to tailor the explanation to the exact clause; only the flagged clause is sent, never the whole document.
